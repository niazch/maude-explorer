            How to read these numbers

            • MAUDE collects reports from manufacturers, hospitals, patients and others. Many events are never reported, and some are reported more than once.
            • “Reported more often than expected” compares this device type with all other device types. It is a reason to look more closely, not a proof of risk.
            • Counts change with recalls, lawsuits, news and changes of the problem codes. Use the Trends tab before you compare years.
            • A report can name more than one problem and more than one outcome, so the shares do not add up to 100%.

            Volcano plot: size of the difference and strength of the evidence

            Each point is one problem code. Right of the dashed line: reported more often than for other device types. Above the orange line: q below 0.05. Point size: number of reports. Points at the top: q below 1e-50. Select a point to open the problem.

            Select a problem

            Select a point in the volcano plot or a row in the table. This panel then shows the 2×2 table, all measures, the first year with a signal and the signal over time, and it lists the reports with the problem.

            Reports: reports of this selection with the problem. Expected: the number if the problem were reported as often as for all other device types. ROR (reporting odds ratio): how many times as often the problem is reported (the bar shows its 95% CI; the dashed line is 1). PRR, χ² and IC025: the measures of the other signal rules. q: false discovery rate of the one-sided Fisher exact test (Benjamini-Hochberg) over all problems in the table. Select a row or a point for the 2×2 table and the signal over time.

                  Reporting timeliness
                  
    
                  How long manufacturers took to send their reports to the FDA. The report time is the date the FDA received a report minus the date the manufacturer became aware of the event (field G4 of Form 3500A). FDA regulation requires most manufacturer reports within 30 days. The method follows Everhart et al., BMJ 2025;388:e081518.
